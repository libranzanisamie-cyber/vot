About digital lending

What it is, why it can be risky, and how to protect yourself.

What is digital lending?

Digital lending means borrowing money through websites or mobile apps instead of face-to-face applications. You submit your details online and receive a decision digitally.

Why is online lending risky?

Some platforms take advantage of people who urgently need money. Common problems are:

  1. Hidden charges: fees or deductions not explained before you receive the loan.
  2. Excessive interest rates: borrowing costs that make repayment very hard.
  3. Harassment and threats: threatening messages, public shaming, or texting people in your contact list.
  4. Personal data misuse: apps that ask for contacts, photos, or other private information.
  5. Fake lending apps: fake platforms built to collect your data or advance payments.

Warning signs of a suspicious lending app

  • Promises guaranteed loans without proper verification.
  • Requests advance payments before releasing the loan.
  • Does not clearly explain interest rates and fees.
  • Has no verifiable company information.
  • Requests unnecessary access to your phone contacts and photos.
  • Uses threats or pressure to make people borrow immediately.

How can you protect yourself?

  1. Verify that the lending company is registered and authorized to operate.
  2. Read the loan agreement before accepting.
  3. Check the interest rate, fees, penalties, and repayment schedule.
  4. Never pay suspicious advance fees.
  5. Do not share OTPs, passwords, or banking information.
  6. Review the permissions a mobile app requests.
  7. Keep screenshots and records of suspicious messages or transactions.

Know your rights as a Filipino borrower

You have the right to…

  • Clear information about loan costs and charges.
  • Privacy and protection of your personal information.
  • Information about your loan agreement and repayment duties.

You should not face…

  • Unfair debt collection practices.
  • Threats or harassment.
  • Public shaming.
